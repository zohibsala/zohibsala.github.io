Overview

Smoke Grenade is a stylised hard surface 3D prop created in Blender as a personal project to develop and strengthen my hard surface modelling workflow.

The project focuses on creating a realistic game ready asset inspired by modern smoke grenades used by military and tactical forces around the world. The aim was to accurately recreate the overall form, mechanical details and worn metal finish while following a clean modelling workflow suitable for real time games.

Inspiration and Research

The grenade was inspired by modern smoke grenades used in real life and those commonly seen in military shooters and open world games.

Before modelling, I gathered reference images from multiple angles to study the proportions, mechanical construction, safety pin, lever mechanism, engraved details and overall silhouette. I wanted the asset to feel believable while maintaining a clean presentation suitable for a real time game environment.

My Role

I was responsible for the complete production of the asset, including:

  • Hard surface modelling in Blender
  • Creating the complete high quality model using polygon modelling techniques
  • Edge support and topology refinement
  • UV preparation
  • Material creation inside Blender
  • Creating a scratched painted metal material
  • Presentation, lighting and rendering

Key Features

  • Realistic hard surface asset
  • Clean polygon modelling workflow
  • Detailed mechanical components
  • Custom scratched metal material
  • Surface imperfections including wear and scratches
  • Realistic presentation using Blender

Technical Implementation

The grenade was created entirely in Blender using polygon modelling techniques.

The model began from simple primitive geometry before being progressively refined through edge loops, bevels, extrusions, inset operations and vertex manipulation. Particular attention was given to maintaining clean topology while preserving the hard edges required for a believable manufactured object.

The safety lever, pull ring, body details and segmented design were modelled individually before being combined into the final asset.

Once modelling was complete, I created a custom material inside Blender to simulate painted metal with realistic surface wear. Small scratches and imperfections were added to break up the clean surface and create the appearance of a used tactical smoke grenade rather than a factory new object.

Lighting and rendering were configured to emphasise the material response and highlight the hard surface forms.

Challenges

One of the biggest challenges was maintaining clean topology while modelling the mechanical details around the safety lever and pull ring.

Another challenge was achieving a convincing worn metal appearance. Creating believable scratches without making the asset look excessively damaged required multiple iterations until the material felt balanced and realistic.

Reflection and Improvements

This project helped strengthen my understanding of hard surface modelling and reinforced the importance of clean topology, accurate proportions and careful attention to detail.

If I revisit this asset in the future, I would like to:

  • Texture the asset using Substance Painter
  • Produce a game ready low poly version with a full PBR workflow
  • Import the asset into Unreal Engine 5 for presentation

Outcome

This project demonstrates my ability to create detailed hard surface props using Blender while following a structured modelling workflow.

It highlights my understanding of polygon modelling, mechanical forms, material creation and presentation, while continuing to build my portfolio with assets suitable for real time game production.
